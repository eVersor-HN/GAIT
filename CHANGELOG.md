# What's New in GAIT

Built forward. Tracked clearly. Newest first.

---

## 0.18.0 — 2026-08-23

The opponent stops talking. Sixteen new activities, and a bar that shows exactly how far the model is from predicting you.

- **Nobody speaks any more.** Every scripted line is gone — the personas and their taunts, the division's memos and commendations, the file it kept on you, the horde's sounds, the stake announcements, the idle pings and the closing line after a session. The opponent now communicates through the only things that were ever real: its forecast, the ledger, its row on the board, and the distance between you.
- **A session closes on a number** instead of a remark: how far ahead of or behind the forecast you finished.
- **Model calibration on the forecast page.** A staged bar showing where the opponent stands: how many sessions until it makes its first estimate, until points ride on a round, and until it is at full strength — so a new enrolment can see what is still missing.
- **Sixteen new activities**, grouped by how you move: guided run and Nordic walking on foot; racing chair, inline skating, skateboard and kick scooter on wheels; rowing, adaptive rowing, kayak, para-canoe and swimming on water; cross-country skiing and sit-ski on snow; rowing ergometer, bike ergometer, elliptical and stair climber indoors. Adaptive activities sit with the movement they belong to, not in a category of their own.
- **Units that match the sport.** Rowing reads as a 500 m split, swimming as a 100 m pace, wheels in km/h, everything else in min/km.
- **Gym machines only offer the timed mode** — there is no outdoor ergometer.
- The live feed during a session reads the gap, the mark and the direction. The spoken readout does the same aloud, for when the phone is in a pocket.

## 0.17.1 — 2026-08-23

- Back behaves the way you expect: inside an enrolment it steps up to your list, and only from there does the app ask whether to leave.
- The horde now has a voice of its own — deep, slow and physical, instead of the division's cool synthetic one. It growls what it means rather than reading it out.
- The live track breathes with the round: both markers pulse on a shared beat that tightens the closer your opponent gets.

## 0.17.0 — 2026-08-23

The restructure: your enrolments first, two opponents that stop borrowing each other's language, a monochrome console, and a page that tells you what to do next.

- **Enrolments.** The app opens on your list. Each entry is an activity, an opponent, a standing and its own history, settings and messages. Open one, add one, delete one — nothing else is touched. Everything you had before is carried over.
- **One enrolment form** instead of a four-step wizard: activity, opponent, identity, name — one action at the end.
- **Analysis page.** Your instructions, ordered by urgency, each with the number to hit and the evidence behind it: today's target pace, the distance that makes it count, the review deadline, how many rounds clear the cut line, which weekday is costing you, and when predictability rather than speed is the problem.
- **The horde gets its own front page** — a containment display that tightens as they close, separation in metres, and a signal log. No board, no ticker, no forecast talk, no sentences.
- **GO** sits at the top of the first page. The session starts where you land.
- **A closing line after every session**, scaled to how far you beat or missed the forecast. Win big and your rival goes quiet; fall behind and it gets colder. The horde only gets louder, or further away.
- **Briefing** collects every explanation the working screens used to carry, so the screens themselves stay clean.
- Monochrome throughout — black, white and grey with exactly three signals: one for you, one for danger, one for a confirmed win.
- New app icon: a flat monochrome mark with a single accent.
- Indoor sessions no longer start by themselves — choosing a mode selects it, START starts it.
- The live screen now shows the number that decides the round: the pace you have to hold from here.
- Settings belong to the enrolment they came from.

## 0.16.0 — 2026-08-22

- **Substitution review with a deadline.** When your model gets close enough to predicting you, a seven-day review opens and counts down in the open. Let it lapse and the model is ratified a generation stronger. Beat it in a duel, or drift back out of range, and the review closes.

## 0.15.0 — 2026-08-22

Consistency pass, notifications, lock screen.

- **Level rows, real controls.** Buttons keep fixed heights and never wrap, so a row of three always lines up. Tiles in a row match. Everything tappable answers to the touch. Selections animate, yes/no settings are switches, and one console dialog replaces every stock system dialog.
- **Lock-screen card.** The recording notification became a live instrument: time, distance, your pace against your opponent's, who is ahead and by how much, and what is riding on the round — refreshed continuously, no unlock needed.

## 0.14.0 — 2026-08-21

The dimensions beyond speed — the part that makes cycling, hiking and motor-assisted activities real.

- **Routes are kept.** Every outdoor session stores its track, its climb and its per-kilometre splits.
- **New ground counts.** A route you have not run before is measured against everything you have, and steadiness is measured against how even your kilometres usually are.
- **Motor-assisted activities are judged fairly.** On an e-scooter or e-bike the round goes to a genuinely new route or a steadier ride — speed does not decide it, and the app says so up front.
- **Hiking and cycling get climb** on the forecast, live and in the debrief.
- **Indoor gets its own instrument:** your opponent's finish counting down, and the moment it passes, the distance decides.
- Wheeled activities read in km/h everywhere, and the indoor prompt asks for what your machine actually shows.

## 0.8.0 — 2026-08-21

The division becomes a place you can be fired from.

- **Quarterly cull.** Head count breathes, new hires arrive daily, and every 90 days the bottom of the division is decommissioned. The board shows the cut line and how far you sit above it. **If you are below it, that enrolment is over** — a termination notice replaces the board, and the only way forward is to enrol again. New arrivals get 60 protected days.
- **All 17 personas** for your rival, each with its own voice across every situation — including the clone that speaks as if it were you, and intends to be the better copy.
- **Your opponent has its own row on the board**, standing exactly where the rounds have put it.
- **The board is live** while you watch it: results land at each asset's own training time, so rows move when that person finishes.
- **Tap any row for a dossier** — standing, history, what they read as, when their results land, how long they have been here.
- **Rest & vacation calendar.** A month grid where you can mark days off in advance; when they arrive they are treated as rest — the session still counts, but nothing is judged.
- **Activity picker** at setup, with eight activities, each keeping its own opponent, standing and history.
- **Commendations** from the division when the numbers back it — the company's version of a compliment.
- **Leaving asks first**, and can mute notifications on the way out without silencing the messages themselves.
- Stakes scale with your lead: the further ahead you are, the more your opponent puts on the line.

## 0.7.0 — 2026-08-21

The division gets a roster. You are no longer one asset against one model — you are one of a thousand the division ranks, reviews, decommissions and replaces.

- **A living roster.** Around a thousand simulated assets, evenly split between women and men with a few synthetics among them, each with a temperament, a training time, rest days, leave, the occasional injury and a career that rises and falls. Every fortnight the division reviews, and those who fall through the floor are decommissioned and replaced.
- **The decommissioned are the horde.** The two opponents share one world.
- **The asset board** is the first screen: your row pinned with your rank and movement, the top fifteen with their arrows and status, today's biggest movers, the most recent decommissions, and the next review counting down.
- **The containment map** replaces it for horde enrolments: you at the centre, every decommissioned asset a dot, closest first.
- **A ticker of today's movers** runs under the header on every screen.
- History is deeper than your account: the division has more than a year of it on file before you enrolled.

## 0.6.0 — 2026-08-21

The competitive layer. The previous release made the screens look right; this one makes it feel like there is someone on the other side who wants you to lose.

- **The ledger.** Every forecast session is a round. Beat the prediction and the point is yours; match or miss it and it is your opponent's. The running score sits under the header on every screen, with your form, your standing, and which weekday belongs to whom.
- **Your opponent stakes points on its own prediction**, in its own voice. You can counter and make the round worth double either way.
- **It talks mid-session** — at kilometre marks and on lead changes, never on a timer, with a cooldown and a hard cap per session.
- **Memos from the division** read your standing back at you as a requirement, not a taunt.
- **An asset file** on you: one pointed, data-grounded observation per forecast, every line citing a number from your own log.
- **A direct channel** where everything your opponent has ever said is kept, tagged by what prompted it.

## 0.5.0 — 2026-08-21

The release that put a face on the app. The whole loop already ran underneath; this pass rebuilt every screen and wired in the two phases that only existed on paper.

- **A full console look**, phase by phase: stat tiles, forecast-against-actual grids, trend lines with the review threshold marked, meters, message cards tinted by your opponent's mood, a five-bar phase track.
- **Live divergence.** The track screen now runs your opponent beside you — its pace, its position, and a card telling you exactly how far off its prediction you are.
- **The debrief** compares forecast against actual for pace, distance and finish, and shows what the session did to your opponent's accuracy.
- **The decommission trial.** Once your model gets close enough to predicting you, a single duel opens against its strongest session ever — deliberately rare, with a minimum distance so a sprint cannot count.
- **The handoff.** Win the duel and the model resets, a new generation starts, and it tells you what it learned from you. Lose and it is at its most hostile.
- **The direct channel**, showing every line it has ever sent.
- Setup rebuilt with real selection cards and a preview of how each persona sounds before you commit.

## 0.4.0 — 2026-08-20

A hardening pass: one shipped crash fixed, several features made actually true, and the plumbing a real release needs.

- **Fixed a crash** that hit indoor mode on a fresh install. Indoor now runs without ever touching location.
- **Rest days and vacation are real.** They now genuinely freeze your standing, keep your opponent neutral and hold back its notifications. The session still counts — training is training.
- **Going quiet has consequences.** Disappear for far longer than your own rhythm and your opponent notices, once, and says so.
- **A short stop tells you why** nothing was saved instead of failing silently.
- **Pace is honest.** Waiting for a GPS fix and auto-paused stretches no longer inflate it, and a clock change mid-session cannot corrupt it.
- **A crash mid-session no longer loses the run.** The session is preserved continuously and offered back for recovery.
- **Settings**: rename your opponent, change its voice or the horde's intensity, switch opponent type, and erase everything with a confirmation.
- **Delete a single session** from your history.
- **Proper launcher icons** for the app and the preview build.
- Notifications open the app when tapped.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-08-20

## 0.2.0 — 2026-08-20

The first real build: a working Android app, not design documents. Setup, forecast, tracking and debrief run end to end on a physical device.

- **The app is named GAIT**, and the rival model becomes its core mechanic — competition that never depends on anyone else's schedule.
- **The six-phase loop** designed and built: setup, forecast, live divergence, debrief, decommission trial, generational handoff.
- **Name your own opponent** at setup, and pick from 17 personas, each with its own voice.
- **Real GPS tracking** in a visible foreground service, with accuracy filtering and no background-location requirement.
- **Indoor tracking** that skips location entirely and asks what the machine showed.
- **Every session is tagged** verified or self-reported, and shown as such.
- **Rest days and a vacation bank**, with an honest word if you declare too many.
- **Statistics** over 7 days, 30 days or all time, with a per-session history.
- **Notifications**, including the same-day reaction when your opponent senses you slipping, and sparse randomised pings so it has a presence between sessions.
- **Full cyberpunk-corpo chrome** — a condensed display face, monospace readouts, a grid-and-scanline background and a persistent status bar.
- **An interactive HTML mockup** of the whole loop, and the design documents behind every system.
- Fixed a real bug found on device: GPS jitter while standing still could produce nonsense pace values and save a garbage session.
